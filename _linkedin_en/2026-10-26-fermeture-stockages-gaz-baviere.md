## The text of the post

On 1 April 2027, Bavaria will lose half of its gas storage: 15.6 TWh, and this is no April Fool's joke. It is stirring up some debate, and we had better get used to it, since the future of gas means closing a significant part of its infrastructure. Let us look at it in detail.

Uniper and bayernugs have applied to shut down two former gas fields in Bavaria, used as seasonal storage: Breitbrunn and Wolfersberg. For the coming winter, Uniper has sold only 5.3 of Breitbrunn's 11.4 TWh. This is not a political decision: nobody pays to fill them any more, but the German state owns 99 % of Uniper, which is pushing for the closure, and Munich opposes it. The Bavarian economy ministry paid the engineering firm ILF for a study, presented in Vienna last week (I was there!). To find a problem, it stacks up the 1995-1996 winter, the coldest in its series, the absence of the Austrian storage sites and 25 GW of additional gas power plants.

The fear is not unfounded, though. Bavaria will not run short of stored gas; it may run short of withdrawal rate on a very cold day, and it will depend on Austrian storage with no contract to guarantee it. The two sites provide a third of Bavaria's withdrawal capacity. The Haidach and 7Fields storage sites, in Austria, make up for the volume, but the German-Austrian agreement on their use in a crisis expired at the end of 2025. And Tyrol and Vorarlberg receive their gas only through Germany: each depends on the other's infrastructure.

Does Uniper have an interest in closing? It holds about 27 % of the storage in the German market: less supply makes the rest more expensive. But it is within its rights, and the trends are on its side. The summer-winter price spread that kept storage alive (+0.5 to +5 €/MWh from 2014 to 2020) has been small or negative since 2022, and futures do not pay it before 2029. On top of that, global LNG supply should grow by more than 100 billion m³ by 2027, even if the Hormuz crisis delays it: a cargo bought in January competes with gas stored in July. Above all, Germany consumed 1,029 TWh of gas in 2021 and 864 in 2025, and official projections take off another 14 % by 2030.

Closing storage sites is the way history is going. The question is who pays for the insurance until then: Berlin proposes a 24 TWh state reserve, funded by a levy from October 2027.

Sources: Bundesnetzagentur, Bavarian Landtag, GIE (August 2026), ILF for the StMWi (May 2026), Uniper (2025 annual report), OIES, Frontier Economics for the BMWE, UBA.

## Code

```bash
python3 prepare_donnees.py         # GIE, SciGRID_gas, Eurostat -> tables et réseau élagué
python3 plot_carrousel.py          # le carrousel du post, deux pages, les deux langues
```

The map network is pruned on the graph: pipes of 1,000 mm and more, nodes within 20 km merged, 900 mm links only where they close a gap, spurs shorter than 120 km cut. Gazelle is redrawn through Bohemia, as SciGRID draws it as a straight line.
